01 Eligibility

UBC welcomes competitors from all over the world as an equal opportunity organisation. Dancers of every nationality are eligible to enter.

02 Age divisions

Every entry is placed in one of four age groups. Ages are determined as of 1 January 2027.

7–8

Pre-Competitive

Dancers should not be performing on pointe for any reason.

9–11

Primary

Pointe permitted at Competitive level if training and age permit.

12–14

Junior

Competitive dancers should be on pointe for classical variations.

15–21

Senior

Competitive dancers must be on pointe for classical variations.

03 Competition levels

Choosing a level is left to the teacher’s judgement. These are the guidelines.

Intermediate

Roughly 2–4 years of study.

Recommended variations
Blue Bird from Sleeping Beauty, The Flames of Paris, La Fille mal gardée.
Prohibited variations
Black & White Swan from Swan Lake, Giselle, Grand Pas Classique.
Shoes
Pre-Competitive and Primary must be on flat. Junior and Senior may be on pointe.

Competitive

Seasoned, advanced dancers — 5 or more years of study.

Variations
Any choreography on the classical repertoire list. Repertoire must remain true to the original choreography.
Shoes
Pre-Competitive on flat. Primary may be on pointe. Junior should be. Senior must be.
Grand Prix
Only this level can qualify for the Finals.
  • No level changes at the door.
  • Dancers may not move down a level within the same season.
  • Soloists must compete in the level they are registered at in the studio portal roster.
  • For a group or trio, the average level of the dancers is taken. In a duet or pas de deux, the higher level applies.
  • If the judges unanimously agree a dancer is above their registered level, UBC may move them.
  • A dancer registered at Intermediate as a soloist may not qualify for the Grand Prix Finals.
  • Contemporary entries may not include acrobatic elements.

04 Categories

Classical Ballet Solo

Junior and Senior competitors must select from the repertoire list. Pre-Competitive and Primary have free choice.

Contemporary / Lyrical Solo

Original choreography. No acrobatic elements.

Ensemble — two or more

Pas de deux, duo or trio. Classical pas de deux is drawn from the repertoire list: adagio and coda only, no variations. The coda is not required.

UBC’s directors may judge pas de deux and duo/trio in separate categories depending on registration, and decide from the number of registered male competitors in an age division whether to split the category. If an ensemble drops below its minimum before the competition, UBC will advise on re-categorising. A soloist may enter more than one solo, in classical, contemporary/lyrical, or both.

06 Adjudicated scoring

Three professional judges score every performance out of 300 points, based solely on the on-stage performance. Each studio receives complimentary video of its students with each judge’s critique.

Every performance is expected to be age appropriate.

07 Awards

Every competitor receives a medal for their adjudicated score. High-score awards go to the top performers, and the judges award cash prizes, gift certificates and summer intensive scholarships.

“The Grand UBC Award is presented to one dancer in the Junior age division and one in the Senior age division.”

10 Music, media & the small print

Music

Competitors provide their own music, recorded at their desired speed and cued properly at the start. No adjustments are made at the event.

Props

Props are permitted, but not dangerous props or anything that will damage the stage.

Videos & photography

UBC provides professional photography and video of every performance, available to purchase. No personal flash photography.

Release & liability

Signing the UBC Release Agreement grants UBC permission to use competition imagery. Neither UBC nor the venue is responsible for personal injury or property loss.

Scoring is based solely on the judges’ evaluation of the on-stage performance — the master class is where dancers are seen by directors off the competition stage. More on master classes →
